The share image, opened and measured.

It doesn't stop at "og:image is set". It opens the image and reads its real type, pixel size, shape and file weight, then says what X, Facebook and LinkedIn will do with it.

share image · what it flags
blocker no og:image, so the link posts as bare text
blocker an SVG, which X's cards don't support
blocker under 300×157, or over X's 5 MB limit
blocker the URL returns a 404 or a web page
fix     under 600×315, so it shows small and soft
fix     square or tall, so previews crop it
fix     over 1 MB, relative or http URL

ok      1200×630 PNG, 60 KB  (this site)

      Fair questions.

      Does my page have to be live?

      No. Point /shipkit:check at the folder or HTML file and it reads the page from disk, share image included. It also checks a live URL, and both when you have both.

      Will it make things up?

      It's built not to. Every fact goes into .shipkit/product.md with its source, and pages and posts may only use those facts. Anything missing, like a quote or a number, becomes a [needs: …] slot for you.

      Does it work on a private repo?

      Yes. Ship Kit has no server. It runs inside your own Claude Code session, and the check only sends GET requests to your own public site, or none at all when it reads local files.

      Why not just ask Claude Code?

      You can, and Ship Kit runs inside it. What it adds is what a prompt doesn't have: a probe that fetches your page and opens the share image to measure its real type, size and weight; a 24-item checklist built from a real launch; a linter for AI tells that runs as code; a separate critic agent that scores each post as that platform's crowd; and dated rules for each subreddit and platform.

      What does the check look at?

      24 items in seven groups: trust, the first five seconds (including the share preview and its image), the offer, measurement, distribution, findability and launch week. The full list is above, and the report lists every one with its verdict and evidence.

      Does it post for me?

      No. It never posts, sends, submits, deploys or signs up for anything. It writes files in .shipkit/ and you post from your own accounts. That keeps your accounts safe and the posts yours.

      Does it submit to directories for me?

      No. Pro writes each directory's copy at the right length, with the character counts, and a dated list of where to submit, with each submit link and its cost. You submit from your own accounts.

      What do I need?

      Claude Code on any plan, and Node 18 or newer. The scripts have no dependencies and only use free public sources. There's nothing else to pay for.

      Will Reddit remove an AI-written post?

      Some subreddits will: many now remove AI-written posts, and a few ban promoting content tools at all. Ship Kit tells you which ones, from rules it dates and re-checks. It never tries to make AI text pass as yours. It drafts, then asks you to put it in your own words where a community requires it.

      Is it only for Mac apps?

      No. It was tested on a Mac app first, but it works on any repo: web apps, SaaS, CLIs, libraries, plugins and mobile apps.

      Why not use a free skill pack?

      Use them; they're good libraries of frameworks. Ship Kit is narrower. It looks at your actual repo and live site, follows each platform's current rules, and hands you a finished, dated launch.

      How does the price ladder work?

      Copies 1 to 25 are $9. Copies 26 to 75 are $19. After that it's $29. Once copies sell, this page shows how many are left at each price.

      What does Pro do that the free version doesn't?

      The free version finds the problems and checks your drafts. Pro writes the launch: posts for X, Reddit, Show HN and Product Hunt in your voice, a dated run sheet and launch kit, 20 real people to message, a morning brief that watches your live site in launch week, a weekly SEO page and a results recap.

      Can I use it for clients?

      Yes. Pro covers every product you make, client work included. If several people on a team use it, Studio covers five.

      What if it's not for me?
